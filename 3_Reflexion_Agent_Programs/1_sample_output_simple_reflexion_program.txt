--- FINAL ANSWER ---
Exercise is beneficial because it triggers adaptive changes across nearly every body system, making them work more efficiently and resiliently.

- Heart and blood vessels: Strengthens the heart, increases stroke volume, lowers resting heart rate and blood pressure, improves blood vessel function, raises HDL and lowers harmful lipids, reducing atherosclerosis and cardiovascular risk.
- Metabolism: Increases insulin sensitivity and muscle glucose uptake, builds mitochondria, improves fat burning, reduces visceral fat, and helps manage weight, lowering risk of type 2 diabetes and metabolic syndrome.
- Muscles, bones, and joints: Builds strength and endurance, preserves muscle mass, stimulates bone formation and density, improves balance, flexibility, and joint stability—reducing falls, injuries, and back pain, and maintaining independence with age.
- Brain and mental health: Boosts mood and stress resilience (endorphins, serotonin, dopamine), increases BDNF and neuroplasticity, improves cognition and memory, and enhances sleep quality; lowers risk and symptoms of anxiety and depression.
- Immune and inflammation: Lowers chronic inflammation and improves immune surveillance, leading to fewer infections and better recovery.
- Hormonal regulation: Stabilizes the stress response (HPA axis), improves appetite regulation (leptin/ghrelin), supports healthier levels of insulin and sex hormones.
- Longevity and disease prevention: Reduces risk of heart disease, stroke, hypertension, type 2 diabetes, several cancers (e.g., colon, breast), osteoporosis, dementia, and frailty; associated with longer lifespan and healthspan.
- Other benefits: Improves gut motility and microbiome diversity, skin and vascular health, sexual function, and overall energy.

Both aerobic activity and resistance training matter, and flexibility/balance work adds protection against falls. Even small amounts help, but the strongest evidence supports about 150–300 minutes/week of moderate activity or 75–150 minutes vigorous, plus muscle-strengthening on 2+ days. For most people, benefits vastly outweigh risks when activity is increased gradually.
